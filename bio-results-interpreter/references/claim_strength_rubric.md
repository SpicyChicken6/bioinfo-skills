# Claim Strength Rubric

## Directly supported result

Use when the claim is directly visible in a provided table, figure, statistic, or user-provided result statement.

Example wording:

- "Gene set X is enriched in condition A relative to condition B."
- "Cluster 4 shows higher expression of marker Y."

## Reasonable interpretation

Use when the claim follows from the result plus standard biological or analytical knowledge, but is not directly measured.

Example wording:

- "This pattern is consistent with increased inflammatory signaling."
- "These results suggest that cell type A may contribute to the observed phenotype."

## Plausible hypothesis

Use when the claim is testable and biologically plausible, but needs additional validation.

Example wording:

- "A plausible hypothesis is that gene X mediates the condition-associated response in cell type A."

## Speculative idea

Use when evidence is weak, indirect, or incomplete.

Example wording:

- "One speculative possibility is..."

## Unsupported / do not claim

Use when the provided results do not support the claim or when required evidence is missing.

Example wording:

- "The current results do not establish causality."
- "The data are insufficient to conclude that pathway X is activated."
